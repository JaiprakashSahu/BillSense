# Summary: Delimitation Bill 2026

### 1. Executive Summary

The **Delimitation Bill, 2026** is a comprehensive legislative framework designed to redraw electoral boundaries in India to ensure fair representation based on current population data. It seeks to replace the existing "Delimitation Act, 2002" to address significant shifts in population demographics across various states and Union Territories. By using the latest published census figures, the Bill ensures that each vote carries roughly equal weight, correcting historical imbalances where some constituencies have grown vastly larger or more populous than others. This process is constitutionally mandated to maintain the equitable distribution of seats in the Lower House of Parliament (Lok Sabha) and State Legislative Assemblies.

Central to this Bill is the establishment of a specialized **Delimitation Commission** to oversee the redrawing process. This Commission is structured to include judicial, electoral, and state-level expertise, ensuring that the process is independent, transparent, and technically sound. Crucially, the Bill operationalizes major recent constitutional amendments regarding gender equality by mandating that approximately one-third of all parliamentary and assembly seats be reserved for women. This includes specific provisions for women belonging to Scheduled Castes (SC) and Scheduled Tribes (ST), ensuring that gender reservation does not automatically override existing caste/tribe reservations but functions alongside them through a rotation system.

### 2. Key Provisions

*   **Establishment of the Commission:** The Central Government will constitute a three-member Delimitation Commission consisting of a current or former Supreme Court Judge (Chairperson), the Chief Election Commissioner (or nominee), and the State Election Commissioner (ex officio) (**Section 3**).
*   **Census-Based Readjustment:** The Commission’s primary duty is to readjust the number of seats and draw constituency boundaries strictly based on the latest valid census figures (**Section 4**).
*   **Advisory Structure:** For each state, the Commission will associate with ten non-voting members (five MPs and five MLAs/Members of the Legislative Assembly) nominated by their respective Speakers to provide local insight (**Section 5**).
*   **Women’s Reservation:** Approximately one-third of all seats in the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies must be reserved for women. SC/ST women’s reservations will rotate only within seats originally reserved for SC/ST candidates (**Section 8(c)**).
*   **Legal Powers:** The Commission has the powers of a civil court to summon witnesses, demand documents, and compel information provision. It has jurisdiction over the entire territory of India for these enforcement purposes (**Section 7**).
*   **Repeal of Old Law:** The Delimitation Act, 2002, is explicitly repealed and replaced by this new Bill (**Section 13**).
*   **Funding:** All costs, including salaries and operational expenses, will be paid directly from the Consolidated Fund of India via the Election Commission (**Financial Provisions**).

### 3. Rights & Obligations

**Rights and Benefits:**
*   **Voters:** Citizens gain the right to equitable representation, where their vote carries approximately equal weight to votes in other constituencies due to population-based boundary adjustments.
*   **Women Candidates:** Gains a guaranteed minimum of roughly 33% of electoral seats, with specific sub-categories for SC/ST women ensuring they retain access to reserved seats (**Section 8(c)**).
*   **Commission Members:** The Chairperson and members are entitled to salaries and allowances paid from national funds.
*   **Associate Members (MPs/MLAs):** Have the right to participate in the Commission’s proceedings to offer local geographic and administrative insight (**Section 5**).

**Obligations and Duties:**
*   **Central Government:** Must constitute the Commission via notification and fund its operations (**Sections 2 & 3**).
*   **The Commission:**
    *   Must strictly adhere to the latest census data (**Section 2 & 4**).
    *   Must ensure constituencies are geographically compact and respect natural/administrative boundaries (**Section 9**).
    *   Must ensure Assembly constituencies fall wholly within Parliamentary constituencies (**Section 9**).
    *   Must seek expert technical assistance (e.g., GIS, Surveyor General) when needed (**Section 5(5)**).
*   **Speakers of Parliament/State Assemblies:** Have the duty to nominate the specific MPs and MLAs who will serve as Associate Members to the Commission (**Section 5(2)**).
*   **Public Officials/Citizens:** If summoned, individuals have the obligation to appear before the Commission, produce requested documents, and provide relevant information (**Section 7**).

### 4. Penalties & Enforcement

*   **Civil Court Powers:** The Commission possesses the authority of a court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908. This means it can enforce attendance, examine witnesses on oath, and require the production of documents (**Section 7(1)**).
*   **Enforcement Jurisdiction:** For the purpose of enforcing witness attendance and compliance with orders, the Commission is deemed a civil court under the *Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023*. Non-compliance can be treated as contempt or obstructing a judicial officer, liable to standard criminal penalties under that law (**Section 7(6)**).
*   **Supremacy of the Act:** The Bill overrides any conflicting existing laws regarding delimitation, meaning the Commission’s final orders on boundaries and seat allocation are the final legal authority on the matter until the next delimitation cycle (**Section 12**).

### 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Associate Members Have No Vote:** The ten advisory members (5 MPs and 5 MLAs) per state are strictly limited to advisory roles. They **do not have the right to vote on or sign** any of the Commission’s final decisions (**Section 5(4)**).
*   **Single-Seat States/UTs:** If a State or Union Territory is allocated only one seat in the Lok Sabha, its entire territory must form a single constituencies; it cannot be subdivided (**Section 4**).
*   **Jammu & Kashmir Provisions:** There is a specific provision (currently in abeyance) stipulating that upon the "de-occupation" of Pakistani-occupied areas of J&K, the Election Commission will act as the Delimitation Commission for those specific areas (**Section 3(4)**).
*   **Nagaland & Meghalaya Exception:** For these two states, the "State Election Commissioner" member of the Commission is a specific nominee appointed by the Governor, rather than the standard constitutional appointee, acknowledging their unique state structures (**Section 3 Explanation**).
*   **Small State Adjustments:** If a state has fewer than 5 Members of Parliament, the number of Parliamentary Associate Members is reduced accordingly to ensure all its MPs can participate (**Section 5**).

### 6. Key Definitions

*   **"State":** Expands beyond just the 28 states to include Union Territories that possess a Legislative Assembly (**Section 2**).
*   **"Population":** Specifically defined as the population figure based on the latest published census at the time the Commission is constituted. This locks the data source to prevent using unpublished or older data (**Section 2**).
*   **"Commission":** Refers to the Delimitation Commission constituted under Section 3, including its Chairperson, members, and associated persons.
*   **"Territorial Constituency":** A geographic area distributed to return one member to the Lok Sabha or a State Legislative Assembly, drawn to be geographically compact (**Section 9**).
*   **"Readjustment":** The process of altering the number of seats and boundaries to reflect current demographic realities, distinct from the initial creation of constituencies.
