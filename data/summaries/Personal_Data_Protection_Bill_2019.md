# Summary: Personal Data Protection Bill 2019

### 1. Executive Summary

The Personal Data Protection Bill, 2019 (PDPB) establishes a comprehensive legal framework in India to govern the collection, processing, storage, and transfer of individual personal data. It assigns primary responsibility to private entities (defined as "Data Fiduciaries") to protect the data of individuals ("Data Principals") they hold. The bill mandates that data be collected only for specific, lawful purposes with the clear, informed, and freely given consent of the individual. This shift makes data protection a statutory right rather than just a contractual one, ensuring that citizens have control over their digital lifelines, including the ability to access, correct, or delete their personal information.

The bill creates a new regulator, the **Data Protection Authority of India (DPAI)**, to enforce these rules, manage complaints, and ensure compliance. It distinguishes between regular personal data and more sensitive categories (such as financial, health, or biometric data), imposing stricter rules for the latter. The legislation is designed to balance individual privacy with national security, business innovation, and state functions. By implementing tiered penalties and specific protections for vulnerable groups like children, the bill aims to build trust in India’s digital economy while safeguarding citizens from unauthorized surveillance and data misuse.

### 2. Key Provisions

*   **Establishment of Regulator:** Creates the Data Protection Authority of India (DPAI) as the primary regulatory body to oversee compliance (Sections 41–44).
*   **Consent-Centric Framework:** Data processing generally requires "free, informed, specific, clear, and unambiguous" consent. Consent can be easily withdrawn, and processing must stop if consent is withdrawn (Section 11).
*   **Data Minimization & Retention:** Entities can only collect the minimum personal data necessary for a stated purpose. Data must be deleted when no longer needed, unless retained for legal reasons (Section 8, Section 9).
*   **Sensitive Data Protection:** The Central Government identifies categories of "sensitive personal data" (e.g., health, biometrics, sexual orientation, caste). Processing this data requires explicit consent and higher security standards (Section 15).
*   **Child Data Safeguards:** Special protections for children’s data, including mandatory age verification, parental consent, and a ban on profiling or targeted advertising of children by "guardian data fiduciaries" (Section 16).
*   **Cross-Border Transfer Rules:** Sensitive data can leave India only with explicit consent or regulatory approval, subject to security checks. "Critical personal data" must remain within India, with limited exceptions for emergencies (Sections 33–35).
*   **Significant Data Fiduciaries:** Large entities (e.g., major social media platforms) are designated as "Significant Data Fiduciaries" and face additional duties, including mandatory Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and regular audits (Sections 26–29).
*   **Biometric Ban:** Data fiduciaries are prohibited from processing specific biometric data unless explicitly permitted by law (Section 92).
*   **Innovation Sandboxes:** Allows tech companies to test emerging technologies (like AI) in a controlled environment with relaxed rules for up to 36 months, provided they adhere to "privacy by design" principles (Section 40).

### 3. Rights & Obligations

**Rights of the Data Principal (Individual):**
*   **Right to Access:** Request a copy of their personal data held by a fiduciary (Section 17).
*   **Right to Correction & Erasure:** Request that inaccurate data be updated or that data be deleted. If a request is refused, the fiduciary must provide written reasons (Section 18).
*   **Right to Data Portability:** Transfer personal data to another entity in a machine-readable format (Section 19).
*   **Right to Restrict Disclosure:** Prevent further disclosure of data once the original purpose for collection is fulfilled (Section 20).
*   **Right to Grievance Redressal:** File complaints with the Data Protection Authority if rights are violated or requests are ignored.
*   **Right to Consent & Withdrawal:** Give or withhold consent freely; withdrawing consent must be as easy as giving it.

**Obligations of the Data Fiduciary (Entity):**
*   **Purpose Limitation:** Use data only for the specific purpose for which consent was given.
*   **Transparency:** Provide clear, concise, and multi-language notices explaining how data will be used, stored, and shared at the time of collection (Section 8(2)).
*   **Security:** Implement "reasonable security safeguards" to prevent unauthorized access, loss, or breach (Section 24).
*   **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** Conduct independent audits to assess risks and compliance for large-scale or high-risk data processing (Section 27).
*   **Appointment of DPO:** Appoint a Data Protection Officer to oversee compliance and act as the primary contact for the DPAI and data principals (Section 30).
*   **Breach Notification:** Notify the DPAI and affected individuals immediately in case of a data breach, providing details of the impact and remedial actions (Section 25).
*   **Audit & Registration:** Significant Data Fiduciaries must register with the DPAI, appoint auditors, and maintain records of processing activities (Sections 26, 29).

### 4. Penalties & Enforcement

The bill introduces a tiered penalty structure based on the severity of the violation and the size of the entity.

**Tier 1: Operational Failures (Section 57(1))**
*   *Violations:* Failure to address data breaches, register with the DPAI, conduct DPIAs, audit data, or appoint a DPO.
*   *Penalty:* Up to **₹5 crore** or **2% of total worldwide turnover**, whichever is higher.

**Tier 2: Core Violations (Section 57(2))**
*   *Violations:* Unauthorized processing of data, violating child data protections, failing to ensure data security, or illegal cross-border transfers.
*   *Penalty:* Up to **₹15 crore** or **4% of total worldwide turnover**, whichever is higher.
*   *Note:* For State entities (government bodies), the cap remains at the fixed monetary amount (₹5 crore or ₹15 crore), not percentage of turnover.

**Specific Non-Compliance Penalties (Sections 59–61)**
*   **Failure to Report/Furnish Info:** ₹10,000/day, capped at ₹20 lakhs (significant fiduciaries) or ₹5 lakhs (others) (Section 59).
*   **Ignoring Authority Directions:** Up to ₹20,000/day for fiduciaries (max ₹2 crores); up to ₹5,000/day for processors (max ₹50 lakhs) (Section 60).
*   **Other Violations:** General catch-all penalties capped at ₹1 crore (significant fiduciaries) or ₹25 lakhs (others) (Section 61).

**Criminal Offense (Section 82–83)**
*   **Knowingly Re-identifying De-identified Data:** This is a cognizable, non-bailable offense.
*   *Penalty:* Up to **3 years’ imprisonment** and/or a fine up to **₹2,00,000**.

**Enforcement Structure**
*   **DPAI:** Investigates complaints, conducts inquiries, and issues directions.
*   **Adjudicating Officer:** Hears appeals against DPAI orders and levies penalties.
*   **Appellate Tribunal:** Looks into appeals against Adjudicating Officer decisions.
*   **Supreme Court:** Final appeal for questions of law within 90 days (Section 75).

### 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **State Functions:** The government can process data without consent for general administrative purposes if it serves legitimate state interests (Clause 4, Section 12).
*   **Law Enforcement & Judiciary:** Individuals acting in judicial capacities or law enforcement agencies processing data for investigation purposes are exempt from many fiduciary obligations, provided they follow mandated safeguards (Section 36).
*   **Employment Context:** Non-sensitive data (e.g., name, contact info) for recruitment and performance assessment can be processed without explicit consent if consent is inappropriate or disproportionate to the risk (Clause 13).
*   **Emergency Situations:** Data can be processed without consent during medical emergencies, public health crises, or disaster management to protect human life (Section 12).
*   **Non-Automated Processing:** Entities with fewer than 4,000 data principals in a year, using non-automated means, are exempt from registration, DPIA, and certain governance duties (Section 39).
*   **Research & Archiving:** Exemptions apply to anonymized or de-identified data used for research or archiving, provided there is no risk to individuals (Section 38).
*   **Innovation Sandbox:** Entities working on emerging technologies can apply for a 36-month "sandbox" with waived requirements for purpose limitation and data minimization, subject to strict privacy-by-design conditions (Section 40).
*   **National Security:** The Central Government can exempt specific agencies from data collection restrictions or require data disclosure for national security or public order, though this requires written justification and oversight (Sections 35, 86, 91).
*   **Anonymized Data:** Fully anonymized data (where re-identification is irreversible) falls outside the scope of the bill (Section 2(B)). Note that "de-identified" data (masked but potentially re-identifiable) remains under the bill’s purview.

### 6. Key Definitions

*   **Data Fiduciary:** The entity (person, organization, or government agency) that decides the **purpose** and **means** of processing personal data. They bear the primary legal responsibility for data protection.
*   **Data Principal:** The individual to whom the personal data relates and whose consent or rights are being protected.
*   **Personal Data:** Any data about an individual who is identifiable by that data, either alone or in combination with other data.
*   **Sensitive Personal Data:** A subset of personal data defined by the Central Government based on risk and confidentiality expectations. Typically includes financial data, health data, biometric data, sexual orientation, caste, and intersex/transgender status.
*   **Critical Personal Data:** A category defined by the Central Government that must be processed within India. Exact scope is to be notified but likely includes data with high sovereignty implications (e.g., national security data).
*   **Processing:** Any operation performed on data, including collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, disclosure, or deletion.
*   **Anonymisation:** Irreversible processing that renders data unidentified to every person other than the entity holding the key to de-anonymize (re-identify). Once anonymized, it is no longer personal data under the bill.
*   **De-identification:** Masking specific fields in a dataset to prevent direct identification (e.g., hiding the name), but the data may still be re-identifiable with additional information. This still falls under the bill's protection.
*   **Significant Data Fiduciary:** A large-scale data handler (e.g., major social media, e-commerce, or banks) determined by the DPAI based on volume, nature, scale of processing, and potential risk. They face stricter compliance burdens.
*   **Consent Manager:** An entity designated by the Central Government to manage consent for data principals, acting as a bridge between individuals and data fiduciaries for consent-related requests.
*   **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA):** A detailed, independent audit required of Significant Data Fiduciaries to assess the risks and impacts of their data processing activities.
