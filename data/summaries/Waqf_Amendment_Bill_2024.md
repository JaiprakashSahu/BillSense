# Summary: Waqf Amendment Bill 2024

# Comprehensive Summary: The Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2024

## 1. Executive Summary

The Waqf (Amendment) Bill, 2024, is a comprehensive overhaul of the existing Waqf Act, 1995. Its primary objective is to modernize the management of Islamic endowments (*waqfs*) in India by introducing stricter administrative controls, digital transparency, and robust legal safeguards for property. The Bill amends the law to ensure that waqf properties are protected from encroachment and misuse while ensuring that the religious and charitable purposes for which these properties were originally dedicated are upheld. The legislation places significant emphasis on data integrity, requiring all waqf details to be digitized and stored in a central government portal.

This Bill matters because it addresses long-standing issues regarding the opacity of waqf administration, the lack of unified national data, and the vulnerability of waqf lands to fraudulent claims. By redefining the scope of waqfs to explicitly include the Aghakhani and Bohra communities and enforcing strict eligibility criteria for trustees (*mutawallis*), the law aims to professionalize the sector. Furthermore, it shifts significant surveillance and survey powers from specific survey officers to district Collectors, aiming to reduce bureaucratic bottlenecks and increase accountability in state machinery.

## 2. Key Provisions

*   **Inclusion of Specific Communities:** The Bill explicitly defines and brings the waqfs of the **Aghakhani** and **Bohra** communities under the purview of the Act (Sec 3(i)-3(ii)).
*   **Central Digital Database:** A mandatory central digital portal must be created. All existing and new waqfs must register their assets, deeds, boundaries, and income details herein. Existing waqfs have **six months** to complete this digitization (Sec 3B).
*   **Stricter Creation Criteria:** Only individuals who have practiced Islam for at least **five years** and hold legal title to the property can create a waqf. *Waqf-alal-aulad* (endowments for descendants) must not violate the legal inheritance rights of women (Sec 3A(2)).
*   **Governance Reforms:**
    *   Disqualification of anyone sentenced to imprisonment of two years or more from serving on Waqf Boards or as trustees (Sec 16(d)).
    *   Boards must include at least two women members.
    *   Appointment of a full-time CEO (rank of Deputy Secretary or higher) to manage board operations (Sec 23(1)).
*   **Property Protection & Survey:** The district **Collector** is now the primary authority for surveying waqf lands and investigating disputes. Government land cannot be wrongly declared as waqf without a proper investigation and correction of records (Sec 3C, Sec 5).
*   **Tribunal Reforms:** Decisions by Waqf Tribunals are no longer "final," allowing for judicial review by the High Court. The time limit to file a case with the Tribunal has been extended from one year to **two years** (Sec 7, Sec 35).
*   **Financial Oversight:** Mandatory annual audits are now required for waqfs with income exceeding ₹1,00,000. The Central Government has gained the power to order audits by the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) (Sec 22).

## 3. Rights & Obligations

### Rights
*   **Right to Appeal:** Aggrieved persons have the right to appeal administrative orders (Sec 31) and Tribunal decisions to the High Court within 90 days (Sec 35(e)-(f)).
*   **Right to Be Heard:** Before any land mutation or specific government order, affected parties must be given a public notice and an opportunity to present their case in writing or person (Sec 5(3), Sec 19(b)).
*   **Survival of Rights for Non-Muslims:** Property donated by non-Muslims to a waqf retains its legal status as waqf property, and the Act protects the management of such assets (Sec 104).
*   **No Limitation on Possession Suits:** The Limitation Act does not apply to suits seeking the return or possession of waqf property, meaning rights can be pleaded even after long periods of disuse (Sec 107).

### Obligations
*   **Mandatory Registration:** It is a legal obligation to register all waqfs electronically on the central portal. Failure to do so after a six-month transition period results in the loss of the right to file or continue legal proceedings (Sec 36(10)).
*   **Financial Reporting:** Trustees (*mutawallis*) must submit full annual accounts by July 1st for the preceding financial year (Sec 46).
*   **Administrative Contributions:** Waqfs with net annual income of ₹5,000 or more must pay an annual administrative fee to the board. The fee is capped at **5%** of net income (reduced from the previous 7%) (Sec 72, Sec 33(a)).
*   **Compliance with Inheritance Laws:** Creators of *waqf-alal-oulad* must ensure their endowments do not illegally disinherit women or other legal heirs (Sec 3A(2)).

## 4. Penalties & Enforcement

*   **Criminal Liability for Encroachment:**
    *   Unauthorized alienation (sale, gift, transfer), purchase, or possession of waqf property is a cognizable and non-bailable offense.
    *   **Punishment:** Rigorous imprisonment up to **two years** (Sec 52A).
    *   **Forfeiture:** The property vests in the Waqf Board without compensation to the encroaching party.
*   **Legal Inadmissibility:**
    *   If a *mutawalli* fails to pay certified dues to the Board, their application to the Tribunal is inadmissible until dues are paid (Sec 48).
    *   Unregistered waqfs lose standing in court (Sec 36(10)).
*   **Audit Costs:** Audit fees are paid from waqf funds but are legally capped at **1.5%** of the net annual income (Sec 1974).
*   **Enforcement Powers:**
    *   The Collector and Survey Commissioners have the powers of a Civil Court to summon witnesses, enforce attendance, and demand records (Sec 4(4)).
    *   Board orders regarding property disputes are stay-protected; the Tribunal cannot stay a Board order while an appeal is pending (Sec 48).

## 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Evacuee Property:** The Act applies to evacuee property that was part of a waqf before 1947. Previous entrustments of such property to the Board are deemed valid legal trust vestings (Sec 108).
*   **Good Faith Immunity:** Members of the Waqf Board, the CEO, Survey Commissioners, and their appointees are granted legal immunity for actions taken in good faith. They are designated as "public servants" under the Indian Penal Code (Sec 100-101).
*   **Community Specificity:** Certain Boards dealing with specific sects (Shia/Sunni) must ensure that their membership aligns with that sect, though the broader Central and State Boards require "broad-based" representation including non-Muslim members in their composition (Sec 13(2A), Sec 9, Sec 14).
*   **Fees and Audit Thresholds:**
    *   Small waqfs with income below ₹1,00,000 are exempt from the enhanced mandatory audit requirements.
    *   Agricultural expenses are deductible from income calculations only up to a cap of 20% of land income, and only if the land is not leased out (Sec 72, Sched I & II).

## 6. Key Definitions

*   **Waqf:** A permanent dedication of property for pious, religious, or charitable purposes. The Bill explicitly includes the maintenance of widows, divorced women, and orphans under this charitable purpose (Sec 3(r)).
*   **Mutawalli:** The manager or trustee of a waqf. The Bill tightens definitions, stating that in committee-managed waqfs, only the office-bearers are considered the *mutawalli*, not the entire committee.
*   **Net Annual Income:** A defined financial metric used to calculate fees and audit requirements. It excludes land revenue, taxes, and certain agricultural expenses but includes interest on donations and net profits from business activities.
*   **Prescribed:** Refers to rules or procedures laid down by the State Government generally, but by the **Central Government** for matters under Chapter III (such as forms and certain procedures), ensuring uniformity across India (Sec 3(l)).
*   **Waqf by User:** A type of waqf established by long-standing custom. The Bill clarifies that this waqf remains valid even if the specific usage of the property temporarily ceases, provided the intent to dedicate it for charitable purposes persists.
