# Summary: Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita 2023

**1. Executive Summary**

The Bharatiya Nyaya (Second) Sanhita, 2023, is a landmark piece of legislation that completely replaces the 160-year-old Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860. It marks a significant shift in India’s criminal justice system by introducing a modern, gender-neutral legal framework that explicitly recognizes transgender rights and incorporates definitions relevant to the digital age, such as electronic records and computer resources. The law aims to create a more responsive and deterrent penal system, addressing contemporary issues like organized crime, cyber offenses, and sexual violence with greater clarity and stricter penalties than its predecessor.

Matters of significant public importance, such as sexual harassment, stalking, and marital rape, have been explicitly defined and penalized, removing previous ambiguities. The bill also introduces comprehensive provisions for organized crime and terrorism, recognizing them as severe threats to national security. By criminalizing acts that disrupt public peace and obstructing the administration of justice (such as false evidence or assailing public servants), the law seeks to uphold societal order. Ultimately, this legislation updates India’s criminal law to reflect modern societal values, enhances victim protection, and strengthens the state’s capacity to combat complex and organized criminal activities.

**2. Key Provisions**

*   **Repeal and Scope:** The bill repeals the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (Sec. 358) and applies to all offenses committed within India, as well as extraterritorially against Indian citizens on Indian vessels/aircraft or those attacking Indian computer resources (Sec. 1).
*   **Sentencing Framework:** It establishes a hierarchy of punishments including death, life imprisonment (treated as 20 years for calculation), rigorous/simple imprisonment, fines, and community service (Secs. 4–6). Solitary confinement is strictly capped at a maximum of 3 months total, with no instance exceeding 14 days (Secs. 11–12).
*   **Sexual Offenses:** Rape is broadly defined to include non-consensual penetration of any gender, including the use of objects or oral insertion. Marital rape is explicitly criminalized (Sec. 67). Penalties are severe, with aggravated rape attracting "natural life" imprisonment, and repeat offenders or cases resulting in death attracting the death penalty (Secs. 64–71).
*   **Organized Crime:** A specific chapter addresses organized crime, defining it as continuing, financially motivated syndicate activity. Penalties range from 5–25 years imprisonment for members to life imprisonment or death for those involved in death-related offenses, accompanied by substantial fines (Secs. 111–112).
*   **Public Peace and Disruption:** Laws against riots, unlawful assemblies, and promoting communal disharmony are strengthened. Promoting hate or disharmony via electronic or physical means is now a specific offense (Secs. 190–196).
*   **Abetment and Conspiracy:** Liability for abetting (instigating or assisting) is expanded to include cross-border activities and acts by legally incapable persons. Abettors face penalties that can be up to half the maximum term for the uncommitted offense (Secs. 46–57).
*   **Protection of Victims:** Strict confidentiality laws prohibit the publication of victim identities or court proceedings regarding sexual offenses, with violations punishable by up to 2 years’ imprisonment (Secs. 72–73).
*   **Offenses Against Public Servants:** Deliberate misconduct by public servants (e.g., falsifying records, illegal confinement, aiding escape) is strictly penalized, with negligence in allowing escapes also being a crime (Secs. 198–261).

**3. Rights & Obligations**

*   **Rights of Individuals:**
    *   **Gender-Neutral Application:** Legal definitions apply equally to male, female, and transgender persons (Sec. 2).
    *   **Defense Mechanisms:** Individuals retain rights to defenses such as mistake of fact, legal compulsion, and private defense. Private defense allows for causing death only in response to grave offenses that reasonably cause fear of death or grievous hurt (Secs. 14–44).
    *   **Victim Confidentiality:** Victims of sexual offenses have a protected right to anonymity regarding their identity and court proceedings (Secs. 72–73).
    *   **Capacity Exemptions:** Children under 7 are absolutely exempt from criminal liability. Children aged 7–12 are exempt if they lack the maturity to understand the consequences of their actions (Sec. 14).
*   **Obligations of Individuals:**
    *   **Obedience to Law:** Citizens must appear when summons are issued, produce required documents if court-ordered, and comply with lawful orders from public servants (Secs. 208–212, 220).
    *   **Duty to Disperse Riots:** Landowners occupying property have a specific obligation to use all necessary means to disperse a riot occurring on their land; failure to do so attracts a fine (Sec. 193).
    *   **Truthfulness:** Individuals are obligated to provide true information to public servants and avoid submitting false records, especially in judicial or administrative contexts (Secs. 201, 256).
*   **Obligations of Public Servants:**
    *   **Due Diligence:** Public servants must act according to the law, avoid prejudicial investigation methods, and ensure the safety of prisoners (Secs. 198–199, 261).
    *   **Accurate Recording:** It is a criminal offense for public servants to intentionally record false information or aid in the escape of accused or convicted persons (Secs. 256, 259–260).

**4. Penalties & Enforcement**

*   **Sexual Violence:**
    *   Basic rape carries a punishment of 10 years to life imprisonment (Sec. 64).
    *   Aggravated rape (involving public servants, communal violence, etc.) mandates "natural life" imprisonment (Sec. 65).
    *   Rape resulting in death or a permanent vegetative state, or repeat offenses, attracts life imprisonment or the death penalty (Secs. 66, 71).
    *   Stalking and sexual harassment carry penalties of up to 3–5 years (Secs. 75, 78).
*   **Violence and Hurt:**
    *   Simple hurt: Up to 1 year imprisonment or fine (Sec. 115).
    *   Grievous hurt: Up to 7 years, escalating to minimum 10 years for permanent disability or act in question of prejudice (Secs. 117–118).
    *   Administering poison: Up to 10 years (Sec. 123).
*   **Public Order and Justice:**
    *   Rioting: Up to 2 years, or 5 years if armed (Sec. 191).
    *   False Evidence: Penalized under general evidence offenses to maintain judicial integrity.
    *   Obstruction of Public Servants: Up to 3 years for assaulting or obstructing public servants suppressing riots (Sec. 195).
*   **Organized Crime:**
    *   Perpetrators/Abettors: 5–25 years imprisonment and fines up to ₹5 Lakh; up to death/life and ₹10 Lakh fine if death is involved (Sec. 111).
    *   Petty Organized Crime: 1–7 years imprisonment (Sec. 112).
*   **Miscellaneous Offenses:**
    *   Abetment (uncommitted crime): One-fourth to one-half of the maximum term for the completed crime (Sec. 55).
    *   Attempted Offenses: Up to half the maximum penalty of the completed offense (Sec. 62).
    *   Non-compliance (Summons/Proclamations): Up to 6 months/₹10,000 for summons; up to 7 years for failure to appear as a Proclaimed Offender (Secs. 208–209).
*   **Enforcement Mechanics:**
    *   Courts have discretion to impose rigorous (hard labor, stricter conditions) or simple imprisonment for the same term, unless the law specifies otherwise (Sec. 7).
    *   If a fine cannot be paid, imprisonment acts as a substitute, capped at one-fourth of the maximum term for that offense (Sec. 8).

**5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations**

*   **Child Immunity:** Absolute immunity for children under 7; conditional immunity for children 7–12 based on cognitive maturity (Sec. 14).
*   **Mistake and Compulsion:** Acts done under a genuine mistake of fact (not law) or under legal compulsion are not offenses (Sec. 14).
*   **Limitations on Multiple Punishment:** A person cannot be punished multiple times for the same act, except where the law expressly provides for separate penalties for distinct causes (Sec. 9).
*   **Ambiguity Rule:** In cases of indeterminate dual guilt, the court must impose the lowest of the applicable penalties (Sec. 10).
*   **Marital Rape Nuance:** While marital rape is criminalized, the definition of consent includes an exception for wives aged 18 and older in standard non-aggravated contexts, though Sec. 67 specifically penalizes marital rape, suggesting a shift toward treating it as an offense regardless of the "consent" argument in marriage. *Note: The text states "excluding marital sex only if the wife is 18 or older" in the basic definition, but Sec. 67 explicitly penalizes marital rape. This implies that even if consent is ambiguous under the general definition, Section 67 provides a specific charge for spousal coercion/force.*
*   **Good Faith in Kidnapping:** An exception exists for acts done in good faith by someone believing they are the lawful guardian (Clauses 126–142 summary).
*   **Special Laws Supremacy:** The law does not apply to offenses covered by special military laws or other local special regulations (Sec. 1).

**6. Key Definitions**

*   **Offense:** Generally, any act or omission made punishable by law. In specific sections, it is restricted to offenses punishable by 6 months or more (Secs. 25–39).
*   **Person:** Includes individuals and corporations/legal entities. Agent holdings and acts are imputed to the principal (Secs. 25–39).
*   **Public Servant:** Encompasses a wide range of officials including military, judicial, local, and administrative officers (Secs. 25–39).
*   **Dishonesty and Fraud:** Defined with specific *mens rea* (state of mind) standards, incorporating modern standards for intent regarding property rights (Sec. 2).
*   **Child:** Any person under the age of 18 years (Sec. 2).
*   **Document:** Includes paper, electronic, and digital records, reflecting the digital age nature of the law (Sec. 2).
*   **Rape:** Defined to include penile, object, or oral insertion into the vagina, anus, or mouth of another person without valid consent (Sec. 63).
*   **Grievous Hurt:** Severe injuries such as emasculation, fracture, or loss of sight/hearing, distinct from simple hurt (Bodily pain/infirmity) (Secs. 114–116).
*   **Organized Crime:** Continuing, financially motivated syndicate activity involving cognizable offenses (Sec. 111).
*   **Rioting:** Committing force on one or more persons for a common object or with common participation, where the assembly is unlawful (Sec. 191).
*   **Mens Rea:** The mental element or intent required for criminal liability, explicitly established for fraud and dishonesty (Sec. 2).
