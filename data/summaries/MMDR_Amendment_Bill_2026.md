# Summary: MMDR Amendment Bill 2026

### Executive Summary

The **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2026** is a legislative move by the Central Government to take direct control over the financial and regulatory aspects of India’s mineral resources. Currently, individual State Governments have the power to impose their own taxes, cesses, and levies on mining activities and mineral-bearing lands. This has led to a fragmented and unpredictable fiscal environment, where differing tax rates across states cause market distortions, increase the cost of imports, and sometimes make mining commercially unviable, particularly for small and medium operators. The primary goal of this Bill is to eliminate these regional disparities by establishing a uniform, stable, and predictable national framework for mineral taxation.

The core mechanism of the Bill is the insertion of a new **Section 9D**, which effectively prohibits State Governments from imposing new taxes or levies on mineral rights or mineral-bearing lands unless the Central Government explicitly prescribes the conditions and restrictions for such taxes. Furthermore, the Bill includes a retrospective clause that declares any state-imposed levies that were not yet collected or deposited before the commencement of this Act as **invalid**. This move is justified on the grounds that mineral resources are finite national assets requiring uniform management to ensure energy security, support the goals of "Atmanirbhar Bharat" (self-reliance), and prevent uneven economic growth due to varying fiscal burdens.

### Key Provisions

*   **Prohibition on State Taxes (New Section 9D(1)):** State Governments are legally barred from imposing any tax, cess, or levy on mineral rights, mineral-bearing lands, or based on the quantity, value, or royalty of minerals, unless the Central Government specifically prescribes the conditions and restrictions for doing so.
*   **Invalidation of Un-collected Levies (Section 9D(2)):** Any state-imposed levy on minerals or mineral lands that had not yet been deposited with or recovered by the State Government before this Amendment Act came into force is declared **invalid at all material times**. This provision overrides any conflicting laws or even previous court judgments on this specific point.
*   **Grandfathering Clause (Section 9D Proviso):** Provisions that protect amounts already collected. If a tax or levy was **already deposited with or recovered by the State Government** before the commencement of this Act, it is considered final. These amounts are not liable for refund, creating a clear cut-off point for validity.
*   **Expansion to "Mineral Bearing Lands" (Sections 2 & 3):** The scope of the Act is expanded to explicitly cover "mineral bearing lands." A new definition is inserted (Section 3(ada)) that defines these lands based on specific mineral content parameters to be prescribed by the Central Government.
*   **Central Prescriptive Authority (Section 13(2)(ta)):** The Central Government is explicitly empowered to make rules that dictate exactly what conditions and restrictions apply to the limitation of state taxes outlined in Section 9D. This delegates the detailed rule-making to the executive branch.
*   **Uniform National Fiscal Framework (Clause 4 of Statements of Objects):** The Bill mandates a shift from localized growth patterns to a harmonized national development model, ensuring that levies are proportionate to economic value and that the State acts as a trustee for the nation rather than individual regions.

### Rights & Obligations

**Rights:**
*   **Mining Operators (Especially SMEs):** Gain the right to a stable and predictable fiscal environment. They are protected from unpredictable, heavy, or retrospective tax burdens that could render their operations commercially unviable.
*   **The Central Government:** Acquires the right to uniformly regulate the mineral sector nationwide, ensuring that fiscal policies do not create regional inequalities or distorting market forces.

**Obligations:**
*   **State Governments:**
    *   Are **obligated to cease** imposing any new taxes, cesses, or levies on minerals or mineral lands unless explicitly permitted by Central Government rules.
    *   Must recognize that any levy not yet collected prior to the Act's commencement is legally invalid.
    *   Are obligated to treat mineral resources as part of a "uniform, stable, and predictable national fiscal framework" rather than a source of localized revenue.
*   **The Central Government:**
    *   Bears the duty to prescribe the specific conditions, limits, and restrictions for any permissible state taxation as required by Section 9D(1).
    *   Must define the parameters for "mineral bearing lands" based on mineral content.

### Penalties & Enforcement

The Bill does not explicitly list new criminal penalties or fines for violation. Instead, it creates **civil and administrative liabilities** through legal invalidation:

*   **Legal Invalidity:** The primary enforcement mechanism is that non-compliant state levies are declared **invalid at all material times**. This means any demand for such taxes is legally unenforceable.
*   **No Refund Liability:** For amounts already collected and deposited with the State before the Act's commencement, the Bill explicitly states they are **not liable to be refunded**. This limits the financial exposure of State Governments to only the levies they had not yet collected.
*   **Override Clause:** The invalidation in Section 9D(2) specifically overrides "other laws or court judgments," ensuring that prior legal precedents supporting state taxation cannot be used to enforce these invalid levies.

### Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Grandfathering for Collected Funds:** The most significant exception is the protection of **pre-commencement collections**. If a State had already received payment for a tax or levy before the Amendment Act came into force, that transaction is final and the money is not returned.
*   **Exclusion of Mineral Oils:** As per Section 3 of the principal 1957 Act, the term "minerals" **explicitly excludes mineral oils** (like crude oil and natural gas). Therefore, this Bill does not apply to the oil and gas sector.
*   **Central Prescriptive Discretion:** The restriction on state taxes is not absolute; it is subject to conditions "prescribed by the Central Government." This means the Central Government retains the power to allow specific taxes if it chooses to prescribe the rules for them in the future.
*   **Financial Neutrality:** The Financial Memorandum confirms that this Bill will **not result in any recurring or non-recurring expenditure** from the Consolidated Fund of India, meaning it is not an expenditure-side bill but a regulatory restructuring.

### Key Definitions

*   **Mineral Bearing Lands:** Newly defined under Section 3(ada) as lands characterized by specific mineral content parameters. These parameters are to be regulated and set by the Central Government, bringing these lands explicitly under the scope of the Act.
*   **Minerals:** Defined in the 1957 Act (and maintained here) as excluding "mineral oils." This is a critical limitation confirming the Bill does not touch the petroleum sector.
*   **Levy, Cess, or Tax:** Broadly interpreted to include any financial charge imposed by a State Government on mineral rights, the land itself, or the quantity/value of minerals extracted, regardless of the specific terminology used by the State.
