# Comprehensive Summary: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Bill, 2022

## 1. Executive Summary

The **Energy Conservation (Amendment) Bill, 2022** significantly expands the scope of India’s existing Energy Conservation Act, 2001. While the original law focused primarily on the efficiency of specific appliances and a limited number of industrial consumers, this amendment broadens regulatory oversight to include the **transport sector** (vehicles and vessels) and **large residential and commercial buildings**. This shift is critical because the transport and building sectors are major contributors to India’s carbon footprint. By bringing these sectors under a unified legal framework, the government aims to enforce stricter energy efficiency standards where they have the most significant impact on national emissions.

A cornerstone of this legislation is the establishment of a legal framework for a **domestic Carbon Credit Trading Scheme**. The bill defines carbon credits, empowers the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) to manage the scheme, and allows entities to buy and sell certificates to meet compliance requirements. This market-based mechanism is designed to incentivize private investment in clean energy technologies, such as green hydrogen and renewable sources. The bill aligns India’s domestic laws with its international climate commitments, specifically the "Panchamrit" targets from COP-26 and the National Green Hydrogen Mission, ensuring that the nation moves toward decarbonization in a structured, regulated manner.

## 2. Key Provisions

*   **Inclusion of Transport Sector:** The bill explicitly defines "vehicle" and "vessel," bringing cars, commercial vehicles, ships, and boats under energy conservation regulations for the first time.
*   **Expanded Definition of "Building":** The definition of a regulated building now includes structures with a minimum connected load of **100 kW** or contract demand of **120 kVA**. This effectively brings large commercial, office, and residential buildings into the compliance net, though states can set lower thresholds.
*   **Carbon Credit Market Framework:** New definitions are introduced for "carbon credit certificate" and "carbon credit trading scheme." The Central Government is empowered to issue these certificates, and registered entities can participate in buying and selling them voluntarily or to meet compliance norms.
*   **Linkage to Non-Fossil Fuel consumption:** The Bureau is now mandated to recommend minimum consumption shares of non-fossil sources (renewables/biomass) for designated consumers.
*   **Enhanced Powers for the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE):** The BEE’s role expands to include empaneling technical experts for carbon credit schemes, authorizing domestic or foreign agencies to perform testing, and collaborating with international organizations.
*   **Stricter Building Codes:** Terminology has been updated from "energy conservation building codes to **energy conservation and sustainable building codes**," shifting the focus from pure energy savings to broader environmental sustainability.
*   **Mandatory Compliance for Buildings:** Owners and occupiers of designated buildings are legally directed to comply with these new sustainable codes, which State Governments must enforce through local bye-laws.
*   **State Energy Conservation Funds:** Each State Government is mandated to establish a dedicated fund to finance energy conservation activities, funded by grants, loans, and agency fees.

## 3. Rights & Obligations

### Rights
*   **Registered Entities:** Entities registered under the carbon credit trading scheme have the right to buy and sell carbon credit certificates issued by the Central Government or authorized agencies.
*   **Voluntary Participation:** Any person may voluntarily purchase energy saving certificates or carbon credits, even if not strictly mandated to do so by their sector.
*   **State Commissions:** State Commissions have the right to make regulations regarding their own application procedures and fees, subject to legislative oversight.

### Obligations
*   **Building Owners/Occupiers:** Owners and occupiers of buildings meeting the new size/load thresholds (100 kW/120 kVA) must comply with "energy conservation and sustainable building codes."
*   **Designated Consumers (Industry/Building/Transport):**
    *   Must appoint an **Energy Manager**.
    *   Must conduct energy audits by accredited **Energy Auditors** at specified intervals.
    *   Must submit annual reports on energy consumption status to the designated agency.
    *   Must meet prescribed energy consumption norms.
    *   Must meet minimum consumption targets for non-fossil fuel energy sources.
*   **Manufacturers:** Must ensure their products (appliance, vehicles, etc.) meet the specified energy efficiency standards. Failure to do so can lead to prohibitions on sale or import.
*   **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE):**
    *   Must recommend standards for energy consumption.
    *   Must manage the empaneling of experts for the carbon credit scheme.
    *   Must identify and designate Energy Intensive Industries (EIIs).
    *   Must ensure name protection (no one else can use a name resembling the Bureau's).
*   **State Governments:**
    *   Must enforce sustainable building codes via state bye-laws.
    *   Must establish and administer the State Energy Conservation Fund.
    *   Must prescribe fees for designated agencies’ services.

## 4. Penalties & Enforcement

The bill introduces a robust, tiered penalty structure to ensure compliance. Penalties can be recovered as if they were arrears of land revenue (a strict legal mechanism for debt recovery).

| Violation Type | Penalty Amount |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General Non-Compliance** (Failure to follow energy plans/reporting under Sec 14 & 15) | Up to **₹10 Lakh**. <br>+ Additional **₹10,000 per day** for continuing violation. |
| **Inefficient Appliances/Equipment** | Base penalty of **₹10 Lakh**. <br>+ **₹2,000 to ₹5,000 per unit** of non-compliant item. |
| **Vehicle Manufacturers** (Fuel efficiency norm breach) | **₹25,000 per vehicle** if deficiency is up to 0.2 L/100 km.<br>**₹50,000 per vehicle** if deficiency exceeds 0.2 L/100 km. |
| **Excess Energy Consumption** (Industrial units/Vessels exceeding norms) | Up to **₹10 Lakh**. <br>+ Additional penalty equal to **twice the price** of the excess oil-equivalent energy consumed. |
| **Ignoring Directives** (Failure to follow orders under Sec 14(n)/(x)) | Up to **₹10 Lakh** per instance. <br>+ Additional penalty of **twice the value** of excess energy consumed. |
| **Information Non-Disclosure** / Name Infringement | Up to **₹50,000** for first offense. <br>+ **₹10,000 per day** for each subsequent day of non-compliance. |

*   **Manufacture/Import Bans:** If standards are not met, the BEE can prohibit the manufacture, sale, purchase, or import of non-conforming items.
*   **Closure Orders:** Industrial units failing to meet energy consumption norms can be mandated to close after a **two-year** transition period post-notification of standards.
*   **Grace Periods:**
    *   **Equipment/Vehicles:** Minimum 6-month gap between notifying new standards and enforcing prohibitions (extendable by another 6 months for market/tech reasons).
    *   **Historical Non-Compliance:** No penalties for failures occurring in the first 5 years from the commencement of the principal Act.

## 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **State Discretion on Building Thresholds:** While the central threshold for "buildings" is 100 kW/120 kVA, **State Governments may specify lower thresholds**, allowing for more aggressive local enforcement if desired.
*   **Voluntary Nature of Carbon Credits:** While the *scheme* is mandatory for designated consumers to meet norms, the specific act of *trading* can be voluntary for general participants.
*   **Transition Periods for Industry:** There is no immediate shutdown for non-compliant industrial units. They are given a **two-year transition period** to upgrade technology or processes after standards are notified. Similar 6-12 month grace periods apply to equipment and vehicles to allow the market to adjust.
*   **Consumer Losses:** In determining penalties or liabilities, the State Commission must consider the "loss caused to a consumer," ensuring that enforcement actions account for the financial impact on end-users, not just the violator.

## 6. Key Definitions

*   **Building:** Any structure (commercial, office, or residential) constructed after the notification of sustainable building codes, with a minimum connected load of **100 kW** or contract demand of **120 kVA**.
*   **Energy:** Broadly defined to include electricity from **renewable sources** (solar, wind, biomass, etc.) and traditional fossil/nuclear sources. This explicit inclusion ensures renewables are covered under conservation mandates.
*   **Vehicle:** Any vehicle defined under the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988. Now subject to energy efficiency norms for fuel consumption.
*   **Vessel:** Inland and coastal watercraft. Now subject to energy consumption norms.
*   **Energy Auditor:** An individual possessing specific qualifications prescribed under Section 14(m) of the principal Act. They are mandatory for audits.
*   **Carbon Credit Certificate:** A certificate issued by the Central Government or an authorized agency representing reduced emissions under the carbon credit trading scheme.
*   **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme:** The formal mechanism notified by the Central Government to reduce carbon emissions through market-based trading of credits.
*   **Registered Entity:** Any body (including designated consumers) registered under the carbon credit trading scheme, eligible to buy/sell credits.
*   **Energy Conservation and Sustainable Building Code:** Norms and requirements for building efficiency and green building practices, replacing the older term "energy conservation building codes."
*   **Designated Agency:** The state-level body responsible for implementing energy conservation activities, now with powers to levy fees and prepare annual budgets.
