# Summary: Banking Regulation Amendment Bill 2020

### Executive Summary

The Banking Regulation Amendment Bill, 2020, fundamentally shifts the regulatory landscape in India by fully bringing co-operative banks under the direct supervision of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). Previously, co-operative banks operated largely under state-specific laws and had limited interaction with the central banking regulator. This bill amends the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, to ensure that co-operative banks follow the same safety, governance, and capital standards as commercial banks. The primary goal is to strengthen the financial system, protect depositors, and ensure that co-operative banks remain stable and professional institutions.

A significant feature of this amendment is the elimination of "multi-state" terminology. The bill consolidates the regulatory approach by treating "co-operative banks" uniformly, regardless of whether they were previously registered at the central or state level. Additionally, the bill grants the RBI unprecedented powers to intervene in failing or poorly managed co-operative banks. For instance, the RBI can now directly attempt to restructure ("reconstruct") or merge ("amalgamate") a struggling bank without first needing to impose a moratorium. It also allows the RBI to temporarily take over the management of multi-state co-operative boards if the current leadership is acting against public interest. These measures are designed to prevent financial crises within the co-operative sector and ensure rapid, effective regulatory action when needed.

### Key Provisions

*   **Full RBI Oversight:** Co-operative banks are now subject to the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, on par with commercial banks. Any conflicting local laws or internal society bye-laws are overridden by this Act (Section 4A, Section 56(5A)).
*   **Unified Definition:** The term "multi-State co-operative bank" is removed. All such entities are now simply referred to as "co-operative banks," streamlining legal applicability (Section 4, Sub-clause M).
*   **Enhanced Intervention Powers:** The RBI gains the power to prepare schemes for the reconstruction or merger of a banking company directly, without the prior requirement of issuing a moratorium order (Section 3, Section 45).
*   **Capital Market Access:** Co-operative banks are permitted to issue equity shares, preference shares, and long-term unsecured debentures (with a maturity of 10+ years) through public or private placements, subject to RBI limits (Section 4, Sub-clauses F & I).
*   **Superintendent Powers:** The RBI can supersede (remove from power) the Board of Directors of a multi-state co-operative bank for up to five years if the management is detrimental to depositors or the public interest (Section 36AAA).
*   **Strict Lending Restrictions:** Co-operative banks are prohibited from lending to their own directors or firms where directors hold interests, with few specific exceptions for government or commercial bills (Section 20).
*   **Branch Approval:** Co-operative banks must obtain prior permission from the RBI (via the National Bank) to open new branches or change locations (Section 23).
*   **Repeal of Ordinance:** The bill formally repeals the Banking Regulation (Amendment) Ordinance, 2020, while preserving any actions already taken under that temporary law (Section 5).

### Rights & Obligations

**Rights of Entities:**
*   **Co-operative Banks:** Have the right to access capital markets by issuing equity and long-term debt instruments, enabling them to strengthen their capital base (Section 4, Sub-clauses F & I).
*   **Employees:** During a merger or reconstruction, employees have the right to continue service under the same terms and conditions. Non-managerial staff or those who resign within one month of a scheme being sanctioned are entitled to compensation, pension, and gratuity (Section 45(5)).
*   **State Governments:** For state-registered co-operative banks, the RBI must consult the State Government before issuing certain regulatory orders, though the RBI retains final authority in most conflict scenarios (Section 4, Sub-clause Q).

**Obligations of Entities:**
*   **Co-operative Banks:**
    *   Must maintain cash reserves equivalent to a notified percentage (up to 40%) of their total liabilities (Section 24(2A)).
    *   Must avoid using "bank" or "banker" in their name if they are not licensed banking entities, and vice versa (Section 7).
    *   Must submit monthly reports to the RBI regarding any unsecured loans given to entities where directors have an interest (Section 20(2)).
    *   Must ensure their bye-laws do not conflict with the Banking Regulation Act; any such conflicting rules are void (Section 56(5A)).
*   **Auditors:** Must conduct audits with the same rigor as those under the Companies Act. Reports must explicitly address whether transactions are within legal powers and if financial statements are accurate (Section 1934(3)-(4)).
*   **Shareholders:** Cannot demand the surrender or redemption of shares; capital can only be reduced if explicitly authorized by the RBI (Section 4, Sub-clause I).

### Penalties & Enforcement

*   **RBI Corrective Actions:** If the RBI determines that unsecured loans to insiders (directors) are harming depositors, it can prohibit further lending, impose restrictions, or order immediate repayment of those loans (Section 20(3)).
*   **Management Takeover:** If a board is found to be acting against public interest, the RBI can supersede the entire board for up to five years and appoint an administrator, effectively removing the existing leadership from power without a traditional legal trial (Section 36AAA).
*   **Winding Up Finality:** Orders for the winding up of multi-state co-operative banks, whether arising from reorganization schemes or RBI requisitions, are declared final in specific contexts, limiting legal challenges (Section 36AAB).
*   **Operational Restrictions:** Banks that fail to meet regulatory definitions or lose their licenses may have their ability to accept deposits prohibited. Existing banks can only continue operating with specific RBI approval if they no longer strictly meet definition criteria (Section 36A).
*   **Audit Enforcement:** The RBI can appoint qualified accountants to conduct additional audits at any time to protect the public interest. Findings are reported directly to the RBI and the bank, allowing for immediate regulatory response if issues are found (Section 30(1)).

### Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS):** Primary agricultural credit societies and co-operative societies focused solely on long-term agricultural finance are **exempt** from the Act if they:
    1.  Do not use the words "bank," "banker," or "banking" in their name.
    2.  Do not issue cheques.
    (Section 2, Section 3).
*   **Naming Exceptions:** The prohibition on using the word "bank" does not apply if it is part of the name of an employer (e.g., "XYZ Bank Employee Credit Society") or if the society is formed solely to protect the mutual interests of co-operative banks themselves (Section 7).
*   **Temporary Branches:** The requirement for RBI permission to open branches does not apply to temporary branches set up for up to one month for specific events like melas or exhibitions. This exception also applies to central co-operative banks operating within their defined geographical area (Section 23(1)).
*   **Transitional Businesses:** Businesses that were already in progress when the 1983 Banking Laws Amendment Act commenced were given a one-year window to complete, creating a narrow historical exemption for certain ongoing operations (Section 8).
*   **Limited Exemptions:** The RBI has the power to exempt specific co-operative banks or classes of banks from certain operational norms (assets, liabilities) if deemed necessary, providing a flexible regulatory valve (Section 4, Sub-clause S / New Section 53A).

### Key Definitions

*   **Banking Institution:** Explicitly includes banking companies, the State Bank of India, and its subsidiary banks. In the context of reconstruction, references to "terms and conditions of service" do not automatically extend to an employee's rank or status (Section 45(15)).
*   **Reconstruction:** Defined as the restructuring of a banking company. The RBI has the power to prepare schemes for this purpose to protect public and depositor interests, distinct from simple amalgamation (merger) (Section 45(4)).
*   **Co-operative Bank:** The bill removes the term "multi-State co-operative bank." All such entities are now regulated under the unified term "co-operative bank," applying the Banking Regulation Act provisions with specific modifications for the cooperative structure (Section 4, Sub-clause M).
*   **Memorandum of Association vs. Bye-laws:** For the purpose of regulatory application, "memorandum of association" is interpreted as "bye-laws" for co-operative banks. Similarly, the "Registrar of Companies" is treated as the "Registrar of Co-operative Societies" (Section 4).
*   **Scheduled Co-operative Banks:** Refers to specific co-operative banks included in the First Schedule of the RBI Act. These banks are subject to enhanced cash reserve requirements (Section 24(2A)).
