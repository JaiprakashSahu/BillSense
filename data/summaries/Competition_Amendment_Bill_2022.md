# Summary: Competition Amendment Bill 2022

# Competition (Amendment) Act, 2022: Plain Language Summary

## 1. Executive Summary

The Competition (Amendment) Act, 2022 represents a significant modernization of India’s competition law framework. Its primary goal is to make the market fairer for consumers and businesses by strengthening the powers of the Competition Commission of India (CCI), the regulatory body that police anti-competitive behavior. The Act brings the legal framework in line with global best practices, updating outdated definitions to cover modern business structures, digital services, and technology-driven sectors that were previously left in gray areas.

A major focus of the amendment is **regulatory certainty for mergers and acquisitions (combinations)**. The bill drastically shortens the time companies have to wait for government approval to merge, reducing the maximum approval timeline from 210 days to 150 days. To encourage this speed, the law introduces an "opinion" mechanism where the CCI must share its preliminary views early, allowing companies to make fixes before a final decision is made. This helps businesses plan their investments with greater confidence while ensuring that deals blocking competition are still caught and corrected.

Furthermore, the Act shifts the tone from "criminal punishment" to "regulatory penalties." It introduces stronger teeth for enforcement through higher financial penalties for cartels, a formal leniency program for whistleblowers, and clearer rules on who can be held personally responsible for corporate violations. By tightening timelines and clarifying definitions, the bill aims to reduce bureaucratic delays and ensure that competition law effectively supports free trade and consumer welfare.

## 2. Key Provisions

*   **Faster Merger Approval (Section 29):** The maximum time for the CCI to approve or reject a business combination is reduced to **150 days** (down from 210). If the CCI does not act within this deadline, the deal is automatically deemed approved.
*   **Stricter Cartel Penalties (Section 27):** Fines for participating in cartels (secret agreements to fix prices or output) are increased to the higher of **3 times the annual profit** of the enterprise or **10% of its annual turnover/income** for each year the cartel persists.
*   **Leniency Program (New Section 46):** Companies involved in a cartel can receive reduced penalties if they self-report their involvement, provide vital evidence, and continue to cooperate with the investigation. This incentivizes insiders to expose anti-competitive behavior.
*   **New "Commitment" and "Settlement" Mechanisms (Section 48B & 48A):** The CCI can settle cases or accept "commitments" (agreements by the company to change its behavior) without issuing a final adverse order, allowing for quicker resolution of disputes.
*   **Technical & Digital Market Coverage (Sections 3 & 8):** The definition of "economic activity" is broadened to include technology sectors. "Anti-competitive agreements" now explicitly cover services and digital platforms, not just physical goods and traditional industries.
*   **Personal Liability for Directors/Officers (Section 35):** Individuals (like directors or KPIs) in a company can be held personally liable for antitrust violations, not just the company itself. However, they are exempt if they can prove they exercised due diligence and didn’t know about the violation.
*   **Open Offers via Stock Exchanges (Section 6A):** Companies can make open offers (takeover bids) through stock exchanges without a prior order, provided they file a notice and do not exercise voting rights until the CCI approves.
*   **Deemed Approval for Filings (Section 29):** If a company does a mandatory merger filing and the CCI fails to form an opinion within specific statutory limits, the merger is considered approved.

## 3. Rights & Obligations

### Who Gets Rights?
*   **Consumers & Third Parties:** Consumers, enterprises, and any other person ("party") can file complaints with the CCI regarding anti-competitive practices.
*   **Merger Parties:** Companies undergoing mergers have the right to receive a preliminary "prima facie" opinion from the CCI within 15 days of filing. They have the right to respond to "Statements of Objections" within 25 days.
*   **Whistleblowers:** Participants in a cartel have the right to apply for leniency (reduced penalties) by cooperating with the CCI.
*   **Individuals in Corporate Cases:** Directors/officers have the right to defend themselves by proving lack of knowledge or exercise of due diligence to avoid personal penalties.

### Who Has Obligations?
*   **Businesses (Mergers):** Companies must file a notice with the CCI within 30 days of executing a merger if their assets/turnover exceed specific thresholds (USD 2 billion in assets or USD 6 billion in turnover, with minimum Indian revenue requirements). They must wait for approval before closing the deal.
*   **Businesses (Cartels/Agreements):** All enterprises must avoid entering into anti-competitive agreements (cartels, market sharing, price fixing) and must not abuse their dominant market position.
*   **CCI Members/Public Officials:** Former Chairpersons and Members of the CCI are legally barred from joining or consulting for any company they investigated during their tenure for **two years**.
*   **Appellants:** Anyone appealing a CCI decision to the Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) must deposit **25% of the penalty amount** before the appeal is heard.

## 4. Penalties & Enforcement

*   **Cartel Violations:** Fines are massive, set at 3x profit or 10% of turnover annually.
*   **Failure to Notify Mergers:** Companies that merge without notifying the CCI face penalties of up to **1% of their total turnover, assets, or transaction value** (whichever is higher). If they can fix this within 30 days, the penalty process may be paused.
*   **False Information:** Providing false data in merger filings results in fines of ₹50 Lakh to ₹1 Crore.
*   **Non-Compliance with Orders:** Ignoring CCI directions incurs daily fines of up to ₹1 Lakh per day (capped at ₹10 Crore). Continued non-compliance can lead to **imprisonment up to 3 years** and fines up to ₹25 Crore.
*   **Personal Liability Caps:** Individual penalties are capped at 10% of the individual’s average income over the last three financial years (or 10% per year for cartels).
*   **Contempt of Court:** Disobeying orders from the Appellate Tribunal or Supreme Court without reasonable grounds is treated as contempt, punishable by up to ₹1 Crore or 3 years’ imprisonment.

## 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Sovereign Functions Excluded:** The Act does not apply to government activities related to atomic energy, currency, defense, or space operations.
*   **Financial Institutions & VCFs:** Banks, Financial Institutions (FIs), and Venture Capital Funds (VCFs) are exempt from *pre-merger* notification if they acquire shares/control via loan covenants or investment agreements. They only need to file details *after* the fact within 7 days.
*   **B2C Arrangements:** Certain direct Business-to-Consumer (B2C) agreements are exempted from horizontal anti-competitive agreement clauses, provided they don’t involve resale price maintenance or tie-ins.
*   **Extension of Time:** The CCI can extend the 150-day merger approval limit by up to **30 days** if the parties request time to fix defects in their application.
*   **Compounding Offenses:** Offenses punishable only by fines (not imprisonment) can be "compounded" (settled) by the Appellate Tribunal or courts, even after proceedings have started.

## 6. Key Definitions

*   **Commitment:** An agreement made by a party to the CCI to modify or discontinue specific practices, which allows the CCI to close the case without issuing a final anti-competitive order.
*   **Turnover:** For penalty calculations, this now explicitly **excludes** intra-group sales, indirect taxes, trade discounts, and income from non-Indian customers. This prevents double-counting and focuses penalties on domestic economic impact.
*   **Control:** A person is said to have "control" over another entity if they can direct or substantially influence its policy or management through:
    *   Ownership of more than 50% of shares/voting rights.
    *   Appointment/removal of more than 50% of directors.
    *   Right to exercise influence via shareholder agreements or other functional means.
*   **Relevant Product Market:** Defined by a two-pronged test:
    1.  **Demand Side:** Whether customers can easily switch to other products based on price, quality, and characteristics.
    2.  **Supply Side:** Whether other producers can easily switch their production to supply the relevant product.
*   **Concentration:** The term replacing "combination," referring to the acquisition of control, shares, voting rights, or assets between enterprises.
*   **Settlement:** A mechanism (Section 48A) where the parties and the CCI resolve the dispute through an agreed-upon remedy, distinct from a final adjudicated order.
