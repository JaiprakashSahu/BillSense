# Summary: Bharatiya Vayuyan Vidheyak 2024

# Comprehensive Summary: Bharatiya Vayuyan Vidheyak 2024 (Civil Aviation Act)

### 1. Executive Summary

The Bharatiya Vayuyan Vidheyak, 2024 (Civil Aviation Act) is a comprehensive consolidation of previous aviation laws, replacing the older Aircraft Act, 1934. Its primary goal is to modernize India’s aviation regulatory framework, reduce legal ambiguities, and improve the ease of doing business while strictly maintaining international safety and security standards. The Act applies to all of India and extends territorially to Indian citizens, Indian-registered aircraft, and foreign aircraft operating within Indian airspace. It establishes that civil air transport is a sovereign function of the Government of India, ensuring a unified legal approach to aviation regulation.

At the core of the Act is a strengthened institutional framework centered around three key statutory bodies: the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) for safety, the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS) for security, and the Aircraft Accidents Investigation Bureau (AAIB) for accident investigations. These bodies operate under the superintendence of the Central Government, which retains the ultimate power to issue binding directions in the public interest. The Act empowers these regulators to issue strict, binding directives to aviation operators regarding safety and security, with non-compliance treated as a serious statutory offense. By consolidating these powers, the legislation aims to create a more efficient, transparent, and enforceable regulatory environment for the aviation industry.

### 2. Key Provisions

*   **Repeal and Replacement:** The Act repeals previous aviation legislation (primarily the Aircraft Act, 1934) while preserving existing rights and liabilities through savings clauses (Sections 1 & 43).
*   **Institutional Mandates:**
    *   **DGCA:** Oversees all aspects of aviation safety and operational regulation (Section 3).
    *   **BCAS:** Oversees all aviation security matters (Section 5).
    *   **AAIB:** Oversees the investigation of aircraft accidents and incidents (Section 7).
*   **Binding Directives:** The Director Generals (or their delegates) can issue mandatory orders to aviation operators concerning safety, security, air traffic, and maintenance. Ignoring these orders is a legal breach (Sections 4 & 6).
*   **Rule-Making Authority:** The Central Government can create detailed rules for aircraft design, manufacture, operation, licensing of aerodromes, and fee collection at non-AERA (Airports Economic Regulatory Authority) airports (Section 10).
*   **Emergency Powers:** During epidemics or public safety crises, the Government can issue immediate temporary measures regarding aircraft, passengers, and cargo, even without prior publication, for up to three months (extendable by another three) (Sections 13–14).
*   **Obstacle Regulation:** The Government can prohibit or restrict the height of buildings and trees within 20km of an aerodrome to ensure flight safety. Owners must comply with directions to modify or demolish such structures (Sections 18–19).
*   **Licensing Control:** The Government can suspend, restrict, or cancel aviation licenses or certificates for violations of the Act or its directions (Section 19).

### 3. Rights & Obligations

**Rights:**
*   **Compensation for Land Use/Shutdowns:** Individuals or entities suffering direct financial loss due to government orders to shut down facilities, requisition aircraft/materials, or demolish structures near aerodromes are entitled to compensation. This can be agreed upon or determined by an arbitrator (Sections 15 & 22).
*   **Appeal Rights:** Those who disagree with an arbitrator’s award regarding compensation can appeal to the High Court within 30 days.
*   **Exemption Rights:** Specific entities can be exempted from certain provisions of the Act via Gazette notification by the Central Government (Section 20).

**Obligations:**
*   **Compliance with Directives:** Aviation operators and individuals must strictly adhere to safety and security directions issued by the DGCA or BCAS.
*   **Avoidance of Aerial Nuisance:** No person can engage in slaughter, flaying of animals, or depositing rubbish within 10km of an aerodrome (Section 25).
*   **Safety Integrity:** Pilots must not engage in "wilful dangerous flying" that endangers life or property (Section 26).
*   **Property Custody:** Owners and operators must follow established rules for the custody, delivery, and disposal of unattended property found on aerodromes or in aircraft (Section 16).
*   **False Information Prohibition:** Owners are liable if dangerous goods (arms, explosives) are found on their aircraft, unless they can prove they lacked knowledge or consent (Section 25).

### 4. Penalties & Enforcement

The Act introduces strict penalties to enforce compliance, distinguishing between general contraventions and specific serious offenses.

*   **General Penalty:** Most contraventions of the Act carry a punishment of up to **2 years’ imprisonment**, a fine of up to **₹1 Crore**, or both (Sections 25, 26).
*   **Aerodrome Nuisance Offenses:** Slaughter, flaying, or rubbish deposition within 10km of an airport is a cognizable offense punishable by up to **3 years’ imprisonment**, a fine up to **₹1 Crore**, or both (Section 25).
*   **Emergency Disobedience:** Disobeying emergency orders (Section 15) is a criminal offense punishable by up to **3 years’ imprisonment**, fines, or forfeiture of the aircraft (Section 15(5)).
*   **Wilful Neglect of Safety Directions:** Willfully ignoring safety or obstacle remediation directions (Sections 4, 6, or 18) attracts the general penalty of up to 2 years and ₹1 Crore fine (Sections 27–28).
*   **Enforcement Action:** If owners fail to remove dangerous structures or vegetation within the specified timeframe, government officers are authorized to physically demolish or cut them down at the offender's risk (Section 28).
*   **Abetment and Attempt:** Attempting to commit these offenses or abetting others is punishable as if the offense had been fully completed (Section 29).
*   **Compounding of Offenses:** The DGCA, BCAS, or AAIB may allow minor offenses to be compounded (settled) for a fee up to ₹1 Crore, resulting in acquittal, provided the offense is not exclusively punishable by imprisonment (Section 30).

### 5. Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Extraterritorial Scope:** The Act explicitly applies beyond Indian borders to Indian citizens, Indian-registered aircraft, and foreign aircraft while in Indian airspace (Section 1).
*   **AERA Exclusion:** When regulating economic tariffs and charges, the Government’s rule-making powers exclude charges already nominated by the Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA) (Section 10).
*   **Emergency Measure Validity:** Emergency rules regarding epidemics or public safety (Section 14) are temporary and expire after 3 months unless extended. However, while in force, they supersede any conflicting rules within the Act.
*   **Delegation Limits:** While powers can be delegated to lower officers, the power to make rules (legislative powers) cannot be delegated (Section 21).
*   **Compounding Restrictions:** Offenses that are punishable *only* by imprisonment (without a fine option) generally cannot be compounded/settled with a fee (Section 30).

### 6. Key Definitions

*   **Aerodrome:** A defined area on land or water intended for the arrival, departure, and surface movement of aircraft.
*   **Aircraft:** A device that can derive support in the atmosphere from the reactions of the air other than the reactions of the air against the earth’s surface.
*   **Maintenance:** Actions taken to ensure an aircraft remains in an "airworthy" condition, meaning it is fit for safe operation.
*   **Airworthiness:** The state of an aircraft being in a condition suitable for safe operation.
*   **Binding Directives:** Orders issued by the DGCA or BCAS that are mandatory for operators to follow; non-compliance is a legal violation, not just a procedural error.
*   **Superintendence:** The overarching authority of the Central Government over the DGCA, BCAS, and AAIB, allowing the Government to issue directions for the public interest that the bureaus must comply with (Section 8).
