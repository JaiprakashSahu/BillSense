# Summary: Insurance Laws Amendment Bill 2025

### Executive Summary

This bill, titled the *Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025* (everyone’s insurance, everyone’s protection), represents a major modernization of India’s insurance framework. Its primary goal is to update outdated laws from the 1930s and 1960s to reflect the current digital and corporate reality of the insurance industry. The bill consolidates and amends several key legal acts, including the *Insurance Act, 1938*, the *LIC Act, 1956*, and the *IRDA Act, 1999*, to create a more unified, efficient, and transparent regulatory environment. It shifts the focus from rigid bureaucratic controls to outcome-based regulation, empowering the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) with broader powers over technology and market conduct.

The legislation is significant because it formally recognizes new forms of insurance, such as health and travel policies, while strictly regulating who can use the term "insurance" in their business name to protect consumers from fraud. It also modernizes the corporate structure of major insurers like LIC, allowing them greater operational flexibility and expanding their leadership tenure. By standardizing terminology and updating definition clauses, the bill aims to reduce legal ambiguity, enhance foreign investment confidence (by clarifying sector caps), and ensure that regulatory enforcement keeps pace with the digitization of financial services.

### Key Provisions

*   **Standardization of Terminology:** The bill updates outdated language across insurance laws, replacing archaic terms with modern, gender-neutral language. It strictly regulates the use of the words "insurance," "insurer," and "assurance" in business names to prevent non-insurers from misleading the public (Secs. 2, 2C).
*   **Expanded Definition of Insurance Classes:** The definition of business classes now explicitly includes life, general, health, and re-insurance. "Health Insurance" is broadened to cover personal accident policies (death, disability, hospitalization) and travel insurance with medical benefits (Sec. 3).
*   **Regulatory Powers for IRDAI:** The IRDAI’s mandate is expanded to include oversight of Information Technology (IT) sectors within insurance. The regulator gains clearer powers to issue procedural instructions through Chairperson-led committees (Secs. 88, 114B).
*   **Corporate Restructuring of LIC:** The Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) is granted significant operational autonomy. It can establish zonal offices and determine its internal committee structures without prior approval from the Central Government. LIC is authorized to set up zonal offices in Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, Kanpur, and Madras, with any additional locations requiring government approval (Secs. 79, 80, 18, 22).
*   **Leadership Tenure:** The tenure of IRDAI leadership (Chairperson and members) is standardized at 5 years or until the age of 65, with provisions for reappointment (Sec. 89).
*   **Capital Requirements for Co-ops:** Insurance Co-operative Societies are mandated to maintain a paid-up capital of at least ₹100 crore (Sec. 2).
*   **Foreign Investment Caps:** The bill clarifies that "Indian Insurance Companies" are public limited companies with a foreign equity cap of 74% (Sec. 2).

### Rights & Obligations

**Rights:**
*   **Consumers:** Have the right to have insurance products clearly categorized under defined classes (Life, General, Health, Re-insurance). They are protected from entities using "insurance" in their names without actual insurance licenses.
*   **IRDAI Members:** Have a guaranteed tenure of 5 years (or age 65), providing job security to ensure independent regulatory decision-making.
*   **LIC Employees:** Have the right to representation in Management-Staff Committees at zonal offices, where union or agent representatives must not be outnumbered by corporate management members.
*   **Chairpersons/Leadership:** Have the right to issue "subsidiary instructions" to clarify procedural aspects of the law without waiting for new legislation.

**Obligations:**
*   **Insurance Companies:** Must adhere to new definitions of business classes and terminate any misuse of terminology. Foreign-linked insurers must not exceed the 74% foreign equity limit.
*   **LIC:** Must establish and maintain zonal offices in the specified five metropolitan/industrial centers (Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, Kanpur, Madras). It must maintain balanced representation in its staff committees.
*   **Co-operative Insurers:** Are legally obligated to maintain a minimum paid-up capital of ₹100 crore.
*   **IRDAI:** Is obligated to regulate and supervise the IT/technology infrastructure of insurance companies, recognizing the digital nature of modern claims and policy administration.

### Penalties & Enforcement

*   **Strict Name Regulation:** Businesses found using "insurance," "insurer," or "assurance" in their name without proper authorization face regulatory action to prevent consumer fraud.
*   **Updated Jurisdiction:** The bill updates penalty jurisdictions to remove obsolete references to the "Tariff Advisory Committee," ensuring that enforcement actions are taken under the correct modern legal frameworks (Sec. 90).
*   **Legal Applicability:** Most sections of the *Insurance Act, 1938* apply directly to LIC and other private insurers, with penalties for non-compliance enforced via Central Government notifications. This ensures that a unified penalty structure applies across public and private sector insurers.

### Notable Exceptions or Limitations

*   **Exempted Insurers:** The Act does not apply to insurers already exempted under Section 2E of the *Insurance Act, 1938*. These are typically specialized or mutual entities that operate under different regulatory umbrellas.
*   **Central Government Approval Limits:** While LIC has autonomy to set up internal committees, it still requires Central Government approval to establish *new* zonal offices outside the five mandated locations (Bombay, Calcutta, Delhi, Kanpur, Madras).
*   **Future Notification Clause:** The list of insurance business classes (Life, General, Health, Re-insurance) is not static. The Central Government retains the right to add new classes (such as cyber-insurance or parameter-based crop insurance) via future notification, which may change regulatory obligations without a new bill.

### Key Definitions

*   **Insurance Business:** Now legally distinct and categorized into four explicit classes: **Life, General, Health, and Re-insurance**.
*   **Health Insurance:** Broadly defined to include not just medical expenses, but also **personal accident insurance** (covering death and permanent disability) and **travel insurance** if it includes medical benefits.
*   **Indian Insurance Company:** A public limited company incorporated in India, subject to the sectoral cap of **74% foreign equity**.
*   **Insurance Intermediary:** Standardized term for agents, brokers, and corporate agents who sell policies, now cross-referenced to the 1938 Act for consistency.
*   **Insurance Co-operative Society:** A mutual entity that must hold at least **₹100 crore** in paid-up capital to be registered.
